About

I research, write, translate and manage projects, in Russian and English. My background is in international history and security policy, and my work has taken me from university teaching and published research to running international research and exchange programs, editing and translating across the two languages, and working alongside senior scientists, policy experts and institutional partners on both sides.

Most recently, much of that work was based at Stanford University's Center for International Security and Cooperation. I have worked as a lecturer, researcher, translator, editor and program manager, and in each role I relied on the same set of skills: build the research from primary sources, present the trends and conclusions in a form that informs decisions and action, and see every project through with attention to detail and an eye on what comes next.

I am looking for research, analysis and proposal-support roles; translation and editing work; and program or project administration.

Skills: open-source research, source verification, synthesis, analytical writing for scholars and for decision-makers.

I have written for scholars, in peer-reviewed articles and book chapters, and for policy readers, in short memos for PONARS Eurasia and CSIS, a network and a think tank that put Russia analysis in front of Washington policy audiences.

Results

Four examples: task, role, result

Doomed to Cooperate, 2012–2016

TaskA two-volume history of the cooperation between the U.S. and Russian nuclear weapons laboratories after 1991, written by the people who took part on both sides, in two languages, with Siegfried Hecker as editor and a small Stanford team.

My partProject coordinator for four years. I kept the work moving between the Stanford team, 117 contributing authors in the United States and Russia, the translators, and the publisher's editorial team at Los Alamos: author communications, tracking every paper through its revisions, collecting photographs and documents, and running regular project sessions by video with remote interpretation. I also ran the translation itself: translated many of the contributions, coordinated all translation work, and edited and proofread every translation through multiple revisions for consistent terminology. I co-authored five of the pieces with Siegfried Hecker, including the essay that opens Volume 2. When Volume 1 was published in Russia, I reviewed the full translation.

ResultPublished in 2016 by Bathtub Row Press: two volumes, 976 pages. Reviewed in Science, Physics Today, Survival, The Nonproliferation Review and Arms Control Today, which called it the standard reference on the subject; covered by Forbes and Stanford News; presented at CSIS and discussed on BBC World Service. Volume 1 appeared in Russian in 2018, published by the Russian Federal Nuclear Center VNIITF in Snezhinsk (Обреченные на сотрудничество, 640 pages). Reviews and coverage

Lab-to-Lab, 2012–2024

TaskGive the same history a home online that a printed book could not: the documents, photographs, interviews and personal accounts, from both Russian and American participants.

My partAll of the content and the site itself. I located, selected and described 86 archival documents (agreements, conference records, memoranda, press); assembled and identified some 350 photographs; conducted, transcribed and edited interviews and reminiscences, many of them from Russian; wrote the introductions, descriptions and biographies; organized the material by timeline, topic and people; worked with a developer to build the site in Drupal; and kept extending it for years after the book.

ResultThe only online collection of its kind. When the project ended, the James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies adopted it and hosts it today.

Sitenonproliferation.org/lab-to-lab

U.S.–Russia Young Professionals Nuclear Forum, 2016–2021

TaskKeep the next generation of nuclear professionals in the two countries working together as official cooperation was cut back: students and early-career specialists in mixed teams, producing joint work.

My partRan the program through three funding cycles under Carnegie Corporation and MacArthur Foundation grants: onboarding, events, research collaborations, partner relations, and the funders' reporting. When travel stopped in 2020, I moved the program online and kept it on schedule.

ResultThe teams' results were published by the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists as two series of papers, in 2019 and 2020, with framing essays I co-authored with Siegfried Hecker.

CISAC's U.S.–Russian expert meetings, 2013–2024

TaskKeep unofficial expert channels between the two countries working, among them a dialogue of senior retired military officers, through a decade when the political climate turned against them.

My partThe Stanford side of the relationships with Russian counterparts, the translation of working materials on government, defense and policy subjects, the record of the sessions, and the running of regular video sessions with remote interpretation.

ResultThe meetings continued through the decade. They are among the sources David Holloway credits in Nuclear Weapons: An International History (Yale University Press, 2026), whose acknowledgments note this work.
